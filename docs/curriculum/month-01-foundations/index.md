# Month 1 — Foundations

The four weeks that everything else builds on. No tooling, very little code — just **how to think about data** and the small amount of math and statistics you need to read the rest of the curriculum without confusion.

## Why this month exists

Most "data analytics in 12 weeks" curricula skip straight to Excel or SQL and treat foundations as something you can pick up along the way. You can — but it makes Months 2–12 noticeably slower because you keep tripping over vocabulary (*nominal vs ordinal*, *population vs sample*, *correlation vs causation*) and over basic numeracy (*log scales*, *percentage-point vs percent change*).

Spend 24 hours here now. It saves more than 24 hours later.

## Month-end mini-project

Pick a public dataset you find genuinely interesting (sport, music, climate, your own bank statements). Write a **one-page memo** that includes:

1. The business or personal question you want to answer.
2. The data sources, columns, and granularity.
3. The summary statistics — mean, median, range, an obvious outlier or two.
4. One chart you drew by hand (yes, on paper) showing the headline finding.
5. Three things you do *not* yet know how to do, that you will revisit at the end of Month 6.

The memo is for you. The point is to practise framing, not to produce something publishable.

## Self-check before moving on

Before starting Month 2, you should be able to answer these without looking anything up:

- What is the difference between *nominal*, *ordinal*, *interval*, and *ratio* data? Give one example of each.
- What does "the median is more robust than the mean" mean, and when does it matter?
- Why is *correlation* not *causation*? Name one famous spurious correlation.
- What is the difference between a *percentage* change and a *percentage-point* change?

If any answer is fuzzy, redo the relevant week. There is no benefit to rushing.
