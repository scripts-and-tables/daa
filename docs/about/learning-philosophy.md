# Learning philosophy

Watching videos and reading docs is the easy part. *Retention* and *transfer* — actually being able to use the skill on a new problem six months later — is where most self-taught learners drop out.

This page is the short version of the techniques that make the curriculum stick.

## Four habits that matter more than the content

### 1. Active recall

After each topic, close the tab and write down — from memory — what you just learned. A messy bullet list on paper is fine. The act of *retrieving* the information strengthens the memory; re-reading does not.

> **Reference:** [Make It Stick](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674729018) — Brown, Roediger, McDaniel. The cleanest summary of the cognitive-science evidence behind spaced practice and active recall.

### 2. Spaced repetition

Review the same material on Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, Day 21. Anki is the canonical tool. Even a hand-written index-card review on a calendar reminder works.

> **Reference:** [Anki documentation](https://docs.ankiweb.net/) — open-source flashcard app with built-in spaced-repetition scheduling.

### 3. Project-driven learning

Every month closes with a mini-project. Without applied work, you will pass the quiz and fail the interview. The [Datasets resource page](../resources/datasets.md) lists practice data; the [Capstone month](../curriculum/month-12-capstone-soft-skills/index.md) frames the year-end project.

### 4. Teach what you learned

Write a short blog post, record a Loom for yourself, or — at minimum — fill in the **My notes** section of each topic page with your own explanation. If you cannot explain it, you do not know it.

> **Reference:** Richard Feynman's "explain it like I'm twelve" technique. Also known as the [Feynman Technique](https://fs.blog/feynman-technique/).

## What to skip

- **Tutorial-hopping.** Doing fifteen "Intro to SQL" videos is fifteen times worse than doing one and then writing 100 queries.
- **Course completionism.** If a course has 80 lessons and the first 30 are good and the rest are filler, stop at 30.
- **Tool churn.** Pick one tool per category and stay with it for the year. You can always switch later.
- **Premature optimisation of your setup.** A free Power BI Desktop install on Windows is enough for the entire year. You do not need a homelab.

## The 6-hour week

The default cadence is **6 hours per week** — split roughly 1h reading, 2h video, 3h hands-on. If you can sustain more, great; if you can only do 3 hours some weeks, fine — just do not skip the hands-on portion. The hands-on hours are where retention is forged.

## When to break the sequence

The default order is the order. Break it only if:

- You already know a topic at the *I can teach this* level. Skip the week, do the practice exercises to confirm, move on.
- You hit a hard wall. Step back one or two topics, find the prerequisite you are missing, fill the gap, return.

Everything else: trust the sequence.
