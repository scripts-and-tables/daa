# How to use this site

DAA is structured as a 12-month curriculum, but you do not have to follow it month-by-month. Pick the path that matches your situation.

## The three modes

=== "Linear (recommended for beginners)"

    Read [Month 1 — Foundations](../curriculum/month-01-foundations/index.md) end to end, then Month 2, and so on. Each week assumes the previous weeks are done.

    Time budget: **~6 hours per week, ~300 hours over the year**.

=== "Track-based (if you have a goal)"

    If you are aiming at a specific certification or role, jump to the relevant track and follow its reading order:

    - [Certifications track](../tracks/certifications.md) — PL-300, DP-203, Google Data Analytics.
    - [Tools track](../tracks/tools.md) — Excel, SQL, Power BI, Python, Azure.

    The track pages reorder existing curriculum pages around your goal.

=== "Reference (if you already work in data)"

    Use the [Glossary](../glossary.md) and [Resources](../resources/courses.md) sections as a curated bookmarks file. Search (top right) is enabled across the whole site.

## What each topic page contains

Every weekly topic follows the same structure so you always know where to look:

- **Learning objectives** — the verbs you should be able to perform after the week.
- **Prerequisites** — what to know before opening the page.
- **Time estimate** — split into reading, video, and hands-on.
- **Curated resources** — grouped by *Courses · Microsoft Learn · Videos · Books · Certifications*.
- **Practice** — exercises and a mini-project prompt.
- **My notes** — author commentary, mental models, gotchas. This is the only original-writing section.
- **Next** — a link to the following topic.

## Conventions

- Every external link includes a one-line **Why** explaining the rationale. If a link looks generic and lacks a *Why*, it is probably a stub being worked on.
- Resources marked *paid* require a subscription or one-time purchase. The site is **free-and-paid mixed**: paid is recommended only when the free alternatives are clearly weaker.
- Time estimates assume you read attentively and complete the hands-on portions. Skimming is faster but defeats the purpose.

## A note on the meta-course philosophy

You will not find a 600-page PDF here. You will find a sequenced reading list, a structure, and notes. The *teaching* is done by Khan Academy, StatQuest, Microsoft Learn, Cathy Tanimura, Cole Knaflic, and others — people who have spent years refining their explanations. Re-explaining their material here would only make it worse.

What this site adds:

1. **Sequence.** A defensible order to study things in.
2. **Pruning.** Out of dozens of "Intro to SQL" courses, only one is linked.
3. **Glue notes.** Where two resources contradict each other, or where a resource has a known weak chapter, the *My notes* section says so.
